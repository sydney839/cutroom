# Planning: brief, plan, hooks and script

## brief.md template

```markdown
# Brief: <working title>
- Purpose: what should the viewer think, feel or do afterwards?
- Platform and format: e.g. Instagram Reel 9:16 / YouTube 16:9 / private
- Length: target and hard limit
- Audience: who, what they already know
- Tone: e.g. casual and personal / calm and factual / energetic
- Must include: moments, facts, people, logo, CTA
- Avoid: people who should not appear, private details, topics
- Music: user's files / royalty-free library / none (never ripped from streaming)
- Captions: yes/no, language
- Material: folders, number of files, who filmed what
- Deadline and hand-off: finished file / package for CapCut etc.
```

## plan.md template

```markdown
# Plan: <title>
Use case: <profile from use-cases.md>, format, target length

## Hook (0-3 s)
- Picture: <what is on screen in the first frame>
- Spoken: <max 8-10 words>
- On-screen text: <3-7 words, different from the spoken line>

## Beats
1. <beat> - C003 "from ..." to "..." - overlay/sound idea
2. ...

## Music and sound
## Open questions for the user
```

For footage-free requests (the user still has to film), write a shooting script instead: hook variants, the points in order as bullet notes (not a word-for-word text unless asked), shots to capture (A-roll, B-roll, screen), and a checklist (light from the front, phone at eye level, 3 seconds of silence before speaking, film the hook several times).

## Hooks

What the first seconds have to do: stop the scroll and promise something the video then delivers.

1. **Value in the first 3 seconds.** No greeting, logo or "today I'll show you". TikTok's creative guidance for ads reports that most top-performing ads put their key message in the first 3 seconds (ad data, correlation, not organic posts).
2. **Three layers at once:** the picture, the spoken line, the on-screen text. The first frame must work paused and muted: show the result or the screen, not just a face.
3. **On-screen text 3 to 7 words and different from the spoken line.** Text gives the what (number, result), the voice gives the why or how.
4. **Spoken hook short:** about 8 to 10 words. At 2.5 to 3 words per second, 12 words already take 4 seconds.
5. **Concrete beats clever.** A real object, an odd real number, a named stake. Puns that need decoding lose.
6. **Open a gap with a clear shape.** Name the category of the answer and hold the answer back ("one setting in Claude that..." rather than "you won't believe what happened").
7. **Statements beat questions.** "Have you ever wondered..." fails. A question only works when it carries a concrete claim.
8. **Never attack the viewer.** "Most prompts fail at one step", not "your prompts are bad".
9. **No invented proof.** Numbers, results and tests only if true; the video must deliver what the hook promises.
10. **Quality gate:** would the creator actually say this on camera, in these words?

Hook patterns for personal or building-in-public content (fill brackets only with real values): found feature ("There's a setting in [tool] almost nobody uses"), self-experiment ("I let [tool] do [task] for a week"), result first ("[Tool] built this in [12] minutes. I only talked."), broken belief ("My app is live and I can't write code"), hype check ("Everyone celebrates the [update]. For you, little changes."), cost confession ("I burned [40] euros so you don't have to"), comparison ("Three AIs built the same site. One was embarrassing.").

## Structure and pacing

- **Hook -> one sentence of context -> payoff -> loop or one CTA.** Link beats with "but" and "so", not "and then".
- **Re-hook between seconds 3 and 10** and wherever attention usually drops: a real new piece of information, not a filler phrase.
- **Deliver value early.** Second-strongest moment near the top, strongest as the payoff.
- **A visual change every 2 to 4 seconds** in shorts: cut, zoom, overlay, new text.
- **One CTA, after the payoff,** tied to the content ("send this to someone who still does it by hand"). End hard; a last line that leads back to the first invites a rewatch.
- **Story videos start at the strongest moment,** not chronologically ("So last week...").

## Words to avoid on screen and in scripts

Phrases that sound machine-written: "It's not X, it's Y", "The result? ...", "Here's the thing", rule-of-three lists, dashes everywhere, engagement bait ("What do you think?"), empty superlatives. Write the way the person talks.
